Warm-Up

What Would You Do If...?

These are imaginary situations. Use would. Work with a partner.

1. What would you do if you found 10,000 yen on the street?

Model Answer
I would take it to the police box (koban) near the station. I think that is the honest thing to do, but I would feel very lucky too!

2. What would you do if you could fly?

Model Answer
I would fly over Mount Fuji first, and then I would fly to school every morning. I would never be late again!

3. What would you do if you met your favourite anime character?

Model Answer
I would ask for a photo and say, "I have been a fan for five years!" I would be so excited that I could not sleep that night.

4. What would you do if it snowed in Tokyo tomorrow?

Model Answer
I would build a snowman in the park with my friends after school, and we would have a big snowball fight.

5. What would you do if you could have any superpower?

Model Answer
I would choose time travel. If I could travel in time, I would visit the future and see what the world looks like in one hundred years.
Grammar: Zero Conditional

General Truths & Always

Zero Conditional Rule
If + present simple, present simple

Ice
If you heat ice, it melts.
Rain
If it rains, the ground gets wet.
Colour
If you mix red and blue, you get purple.
Study
If students study hard, they get good grades.
Plants
If you don't water a plant, it dies.

We use the zero conditional for things that are always true — science, rules, habits.

Grammar: First & Second Conditional

Real Situations vs Imaginary Situations

First Conditional — Real / Possible
If + present simple, will + verb

If it rains tomorrow, I will take an umbrella.
If I study hard, I will pass the exam.

Second Conditional — Imaginary / Unlikely
If + past simple, would + verb

If I had a superyacht, I would sail around the world.
If I were a famous athlete, I would visit my school.

In the second conditional we say "If I were..." (not "If I was") — this is more natural in English.

Compare Pictures: First Conditional

What Will You Do?

Look at both pictures. These are real situations — answer with will.

Picture 1: Rain
Picture 2: Sunshine

1. In Picture 1, what is the weather like? What will the man do next?

2. In Picture 2, what is the man doing? Why do you think he is lying in the sun?

3. If it rains tomorrow, what will you do after school?

4. If it is sunny this weekend, where will you go?

5. Which picture shows the kind of weather you prefer? Why?

Model Answer
In Picture 1, it is raining and the street is wet. The man is holding an umbrella, so he will probably walk to the station or go home. In Picture 2, the man is lying in the sun by the water. He is relaxing and enjoying the warm weather. If it rains tomorrow, I will stay at home and play video games. If it is sunny this weekend, I will go to the beach with my friends. I prefer the sunny weather because I can play outside.

Compare Pictures: Second Conditional

Imagine It!

These are imaginary situations. Answer with would.

Picture 1: Athlete
Picture 2: Superyacht

1. In Picture 1, what is the boy doing? If you were a baseball player, which team would you play for?

2. In Picture 2, what kind of boat is this? How much money do you think it costs?

3. If you had a superyacht, where would you sail first?

4. If you were a famous athlete, what would you do after a game?

5. Which of these two would you rather have — the career or the boat? Why?

Model Answer
In Picture 1, the boy is playing baseball. He is wearing a helmet and holding a bat, so he is ready to hit the ball. If I were a baseball player, I would play for the Yomiuri Giants. In Picture 2, this is a very large and expensive superyacht. It probably costs millions of dollars. If I had a superyacht, I would sail to Okinawa first, because I have never been there. If I were a famous athlete, I would sign autographs for my fans after the game. I would rather have the career, because I could help my family and make them proud.
