Warm-Up

Pass the Message

Somebody said something to you this week. You repeated it to someone else. Start there.

What is the most interesting thing a teacher said to you this week?

Go further: When you told a friend, what did you change about the wording?

Possible answer
My teacher said our essay had a surprising ending. When I told my friend I said she liked it, which was not quite the same.

Who at school talks in a way you always remember?

Go further: What do they say that you could repeat word for word?

Possible answer
Our club teacher says we should finish before we get tired. I can hear her voice when I say it.
Grammar in Context

Said That, Told Me

Use said that when you report a statement. Use told me when you name the listener.

Direct: I am nervous about the test. Reported: She said she was nervous. Now try it with a friend's name.

Go further: What changes when you add the name of the person listening?

Possible answer
He told me he was nervous about the test. Adding my name makes it feel like a private message.

Direct: I will bring the book tomorrow. Change it into reported speech.

Go further: What happens to tomorrow when the sentence moves back?

Possible answer
She said she would bring the book the next day. Yesterday's tomorrow becomes the next day.
Message Game

What Did They Say?

Listen for the real message, not only the words.

A friend says, I am fine, but their voice is quiet. What do you report to another friend?

Go further: What do you leave out, and why?

Possible answer
I say they seemed upset. I leave out the exact words because repeating them would sound like gossip.

A classmate says they forgot their homework. What is a kind way to report that to the teacher?

Go further: What would be an unkind version of the same message?

Possible answer
I would say they left it at home. The unkind version is to announce that they did not do it.

Someone asks you to keep a secret, then tells three other people. What do you say back?

Go further: How do you report that without starting a fight?

Possible answer
I tell them I heard it from someone else. I do not name the three people because that would spread it further.

Speaking Practice

The Gossip Game

One student gets a sentence. They pass it down the line. Compare the last version with the first.

Why does a message change when five people pass it on?

Go further: What can you do to keep a message accurate?

Possible answer
People add their own guess to fill gaps. I would ask each person to repeat the exact words before changing anything.

When is a rumour harmless, and when does it hurt someone?

Go further: What would you say to stop one?

Possible answer
It is harmless when nobody gets judged. I would say I heard it from one person and I am not sure it is true.
Roleplay

News Desk

You are a reporter. Your partner witnessed something strange at school. Ask, then report.

What question would you ask first to get the clearest answer?

Go further: Which follow-up would check the details?

Possible answer
I would ask what they saw first. Then I would ask what happened next, because that shows whether they really watched it.

How do you report a witness who sounds unsure?

Go further: What words show uncertainty without mocking them?

Possible answer
I say they think they saw a bike near the gate. I avoid copying their hesitation and I do not call them confused.

Challenge

Three Versions

Say one thing you heard today three ways: exact words, friendly report, and careful report.

Which version would you send to a friend, and which to a teacher?

Go further: Why does the audience change your wording?

Possible answer
My friend gets the friendly version because we joke. My teacher gets the careful one because I do not want to twist what was said.

Can reporting something change how you feel about it?

Go further: Give an example from your own week.

Possible answer
Yes. I told my friend I was annoyed and saying it out loud made it smaller. The message changed me, not just the listener.
Go Deeper

Meaning and Trust

Reporting is not only grammar. It is a choice about what to carry.

Why do people change a message when they repeat it?

Go further: When is that change helpful?

Possible answer
They want the listener to understand, so they cut parts. It helps when the original was confusing.

How do you decide whether to repeat something someone told you?

Go further: What do you ask yourself before you speak?

Possible answer
I ask if the person would be happy to hear it said out loud. If not, I keep it.

Who do you trust to report your words accurately, and why them?

Go further: What would you tell them if they got it wrong?

Possible answer
My close friend, because they ask before they repeat. I would tell them the exact bit they changed, calmly.
Wrap-Up

Carry It Carefully

One exact quote, one reported statement, one message you chose not to pass on.

Which message from today will you carry into tomorrow?

Go further: How will you check that you got it right?

Possible answer
The one about asking before repeating. I will ask the person what they meant if I am unsure.
