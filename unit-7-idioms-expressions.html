Warm-Up

Do You Know These Already?

These questions all contain an idiom. Guess the meaning, then answer with a partner.

1. Have you ever felt "under the weather"? What did you do?

Model Answer
Yes, I felt under the weather last winter. I had a headache and a sore throat, so I stayed in bed and drank hot tea.

2. Which subject at school is "a piece of cake" for you?

Model Answer
Science is a piece of cake for me. I understand it quickly and I always get good marks on the tests.

3. Is it hard for you to "break the ice" with someone new?

Model Answer
Yes, it is hard for me. I am shy, so I usually wait for the other person to speak first.

4. When were you last "on cloud nine"?

Model Answer
I was on cloud nine when my team won the football match. I was so happy that I could not stop smiling all day.

5. What time do you usually "hit the sack" on a school night?

Model Answer
I usually hit the sack at about eleven o'clock, but I sometimes stay up until midnight when I am watching anime.
Grammar: Idioms

What Is an Idiom?

Idiom Rule
An idiom = a group of words with one special meaning

Literal
"It is raining cats and dogs." → It is raining animals?!
Real meaning
It is raining very heavily. (No animals fall from the sky!)
The mistake
Do not translate an idiom word by word.
The rule
Learn the whole phrase, not the individual words.

Native speakers use idioms in almost every conversation — so they are a fast way to sound natural.

Compare Pictures: Everyday Life

Money & Decisions

Use an idiom from Set 2 to describe each picture.

Picture 1: Expensive Car
Picture 2: Tennis Court

1. In Picture 1, what kind of car is this? Which idiom describes its price?

2. In Picture 2, where are we? What does "the ball is in your court" mean?

3. Have you ever bought something that "cost an arm and a leg"?

4. When was the last time someone said to you "the ball is in your court"?

5. Which of these two would you like to have — the car or the lesson? Why?

Model Answer
In Picture 1, I can see a silver Mercedes. It is a very expensive car, so it costs an arm and a leg. In Picture 2, we are on a tennis court. "The ball is in your court" means that it is now your turn to decide or to act. I once bought a phone that cost an arm and a leg, but I still use it every day. My teacher told me "the ball is in your court" when I had to choose between two clubs. I would like to have the car, because I could drive to school and I would never be late.
